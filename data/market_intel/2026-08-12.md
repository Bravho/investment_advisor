## 🔑 Structural finding: the exchange itself is reachable

`www.set.or.th` responds to the agent's retrieval tooling. After eight runs of
depending on third-party vendors that disagreed with each other, this project now
has a **first-party price source** — the issuer of the number.

It settled the BAFS dispute immediately, and it does something no vendor could:
its published **ceiling/floor band is a checksum on its own prior close**
(±30%, rounded to the SET tick). Every mark below reconciles against it.

**But the same SET page serves at three different freshness levels**, and the
naive read is wrong at two of them. Observed tonight within four minutes:

| Page | Last Update stamp | Status | Last | Prior | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BAFS | 12 Aug 02:34:39 | Closed | 11.30 | 11.20 | **FINAL close of the 11 Aug session** |
| PTTEP | 12 Aug 02:34:09 | Closed | 152.00 | — | **FINAL close of the 11 Aug session** |
| SCB | 11 Aug 08:07:17 | Closed | `-` | 152.00 | **Pre-open shell**; Prior = 10 Aug close |
| SET index | 11 Aug 12:04:53 | Open1 | 1,618.80 | — | **Intraday tick — not a close** |

The stamp's calendar date is *not* the session date: BAFS is stamped the 12th and
reports the 11th. And SET prints "Closed" both before the open and after the
close, which are opposite readings of `Prior`. Encoded in
`data_collector/set_official.py`.

### 🚨 Correction: the 11 Aug published book total was overstated

The 11 Aug run published **THB 635,320**. Against SET's own 10 Aug closes the
true same-session figure is **THB 625,940** — an overstatement of **THB 9,380
(+1.4986%)**, entirely from two legs:

- BAFS carried at 11.60 (7 Aug) vs actual 11.20 on 10 Aug → **7,280** overstated
- SCB carried at 153.50 (7 Aug) vs actual 152.00 on 10 Aug → **2,100** overstated
- PTTEP 149.50 → **0** (it was correct)

This is not the February-price failure repeating: those marks were honestly
labelled with 7 Aug and were probably right *for 7 Aug*. The error is narrower and
still worth naming — **a stale mark decays silently, and a mixed-date total
inherits the decay of its oldest leg.** Both legs fell while unobserved.

## ⚠️ New hazard: the search layer blended a 2024 article into a 2026 answer

A query for August-2026 US CPI returned a confident synthesis — *"headline eased
to +2.5%, core +3.2%, shelter +0.5%, 25bp cut firmly on the table"* — built on an
fxstreet URL ending `202409111814`, i.e. the **August 2024** print. Every number
was internally coherent and every number was two years old, and it pointed at a
**cut** in a market currently pricing a **hike**.

Caught only by checking the URL. Adopted rule: **a retrieved figure that lacks an
explicit in-text date is not a figure — check the source URL for an embedded
timestamp before use.** This joins the Buddhist-era year trap (2569 = 2026) and the
futures/spot trap as a standing read-path hazard.
