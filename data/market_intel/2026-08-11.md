## 🚨 Correction: the Fed narrative in this project has been backwards

Prior runs (Aug-09 explicitly) adopted a **rate cut** as the base case and
logged the disagreement as unresolved. Monday's coverage settles it in the other
direction, and the correction matters:

> "a softer-than-expected jobs report eased fears of an upcoming Federal Reserve
> **rate hike**" … "Federal Reserve officials remain **divided on whether to
> raise** interest rates as they monitor the oil shock from the war in the
> Middle East"

The live debate is **hold versus hike**, driven by an oil-supply inflation
shock — *not* hold versus cut. The weak July payrolls (−23,000 against +80,000
expected) removed hike pressure; they did not create cut expectations. Adopted:
**hold, with a hike tail risk that rises if Wednesday's CPI runs hot.**
This matters directly for Tho: a hike-risk regime is a headwind for the gold
sleeve, not the tailwind a cut would have been.

## Middle East / oil — the dominant cross-asset driver

Iran said an Oman transit deal on the **Strait of Hormuz** is in its final
stages but that the waterway reopens only once the US meets further conditions;
Washington says it is "low-keying" negotiations and Tehran denies talks are
happening at all. Iran's supreme leader replaced the official who issued the
demands with a Revolutionary Guards veteran sceptical of talks — a hardening,
not a softening. Brent **$84.68**, WTI **$79.19**, silver **$64.12**.

**This is the single fact that moves Tho's book both ways at once**: it is
positive for PTTEP (33% of the equity book) and negative for BAFS (33%), which
buys jet fuel. The two largest equity positions are on opposite sides of the
same trade — the closest thing to a hedge in the portfolio, and it is accidental
rather than designed.
