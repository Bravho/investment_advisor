## 1. Headline — a *drafted* 60-day interim Hormuz deal

The US, Iran and Oman are near an interim agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and for the first time the terms are concrete rather than atmospheric:

| Term | Detail |
|---|---|
| Duration | **60 days**, with an option to extend |
| Inbound routing | North, through **Iranian** territorial waters |
| Outbound routing | South, through **Omani** territorial waters |
| Transit fees / tolls | **None** |
| Mine clearance | Main shipping lane cleared within **30 days** |
| After clearance | Two-way traffic under a **permanent arrangement still to be negotiated** by Iran and Oman |

Qatar confirmed an interim proposal had been drafted. Trump said negotiations were "moving along very nicely" and that more would be known "within 48 hours"; Axios reported Washington aimed to announce as early as Wednesday Aug-05. Reports also indicated Iran was considering allowing European countries to clear the mines.

**Read this precisely: it is `deal_partial` crystallising, not `deal_signed` arriving.** A 60-day interim corridor with the permanent lane *explicitly deferred* is the textbook definition of partial de-escalation.

### The counterweight, and it is severe

The same parties reached a **materially similar interim agreement in June 2026** — reopen the strait, run 60 days of talks — and it **collapsed** over escalating hostilities focused on this same strait. That is direct precedent for failure of this exact instrument. A draft is not a signature.

### The observation that actually matters

Brent **rose back above $80** on Aug-05 rather than extending its fall, holding most of the prior two sessions' losses. Pair that with the fact that mass keeps migrating to the de-escalation scenarios and you get the useful conclusion:

> **The market has already paid for this de-escalation and has stopped being rewarded for incremental good news.**

For Tho that caps the oil channel in *both* directions — the remaining BAFS jet-fuel relief and the remaining PTTEP premium erosion are largely priced. Neither name should be re-traded on further Hormuz headlines.

### Decision: do not buy Vietnam for this catalyst

Written up in full as `data/research/vietnam_ftse_2026.json`, computed by the new `portfolio_engine/foreign_access_cost.py`.

| Route (5% of ex-gold book, 24-month hold) | All-in cost | Annualised |
|---|---|---|
| Thai-domiciled Vietnam feeder fund (THB-settled) | **10.80%** | 5.40%/yr |
| VanEck Vietnam ETF (VNM) via offshore account | **12.00%** | 6.00%/yr |

That is the entire realistic return of a diversifying satellite, consumed before the market does anything.

- **Largest single cost on both routes is VND structural drift (~6% over 24m).** The State Bank of Vietnam runs a managed crawling band that has depreciated the dong against the USD in most years (~2–4%/yr). Unlike volatility this is **not two-sided** — it is a near-certain drag, and it is *larger than either route's TER*. Almost no retail write-up on the upgrade mentions it.
- **The USD 15 commission floor is 1.67%** of a ~USD 897 ticket, versus a 0.25% headline rate.
- **Sizing does not rescue it.** Tripling to an uncomfortable 15% of the book only takes the best route from 10.8% to **9.8%** — because the dominant cost is size-invariant.
- **The catalyst has been public since October 2025** and reconfirmed in April 2026. A known-date, price-insensitive passive flow is the most efficiently front-run event class that exists.
- **Wrong instrument for the problem.** It is proposed as a cure for Thai concentration, but Vietnam is a correlated ASEAN neighbour — same export-manufacturing, China-demand, tourism and USD-funding factors.

This is not a view that Vietnam is a bad market. It is a view that Tho cannot access it cheaply enough, at the size he would sensibly use, for this catalyst to be worth it.
