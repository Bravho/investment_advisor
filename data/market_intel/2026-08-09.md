## 0. The two findings that matter

### 🚨 A. The PTTEP dividend alert was never going to fire

The confirmed PTTEP ex-date is **2026-08-14 — five days from tonight**, which is exactly
the alert window `dividend_tracker.py` is configured for (`ALERT_ADVANCE_DAYS = 5`). The
alert should have fired tonight. It did not, and would not have.

`data/dividends/dividend_history.json` **had never been written**. `seed_known_dividends()`
exists, is correct, and contains the right PTTEP record — but nothing ever calls it, and
`_load_history()` returns `{}` when the file is absent. So `upcoming_ex_dates()` returned
an empty list, and an empty list renders exactly like "no dividends are due."

This is **the same failure shape as the dead price feed**, one layer up: absence of data
is indistinguishable from absence of events. The Aug-08 run wrote "already seeded in
`dividend_tracker.py`; the ex-date alert will fire" — that was checked against the *source
code*, not against the *store*, and the store is what the function reads. Verifying that
the right data exists in a seed function is not the same as verifying it is retrievable.

**Fixed and verified tonight** (see § 5). The alert now fires: `PTTEP.BK, 2026-08-14,
THB 4.50, days_until_ex_date: 5, ⚠️ UPCOMING`.

### 🚨 B. The system reports THB 608,580 of profit on a THB 608,580 portfolio

The `avg_cost_price = 0` defect flagged in memory but never acted on has now been
**quantified and confirmed against live data**. With cost coerced to zero,
`pnl = quantity × (price − cost)` collapses to `quantity × price`, so the entire market
value of every position is reported as profit:

| Ticker | Market value THB | Reported "P&L" THB | Reported P&L % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| BAFS.BK | 189,279.99 | 189,279.99 | 0.0 |
| PTTEP.BK | 201,600.00 | 201,600.00 | 0.0 |
| SCB.BK | 217,700.00 | 217,700.00 | 0.0 |
| **Total** | **608,579.99** | **608,579.99** | — |

Two independent contradictions are visible in that table. `pnl_thb == market_value_thb`
is the arithmetic signature of a zero cost basis. And `pnl_pct = 0.0` alongside a non-zero
`pnl_thb` is self-contradictory — both fields cannot be true, so at least one is fabricated.
Neither has ever raised an error, because a missing cost basis was silently coerced into a
*valid-looking* number rather than treated as absent.

This is a worse class of defect than the stale price feed. A stale price is wrong in one
direction and eventually looks odd. A fabricated cost basis is wrong in the direction that
**flatters the owner**, and flattering errors do not get investigated. It has been in the
system since inception and no run has caught it.

**Fixed tonight** — P&L is now suppressed (`None`), never zeroed and never set to market
value. Details in § 5.

## 3. Gold — the mark this project adopted was wrong, and the framing was worse

**Two corrections, one of them material.**

**(a) The Aug-08 mark of $4,401 was too high by ~1.3%.** That figure was an 8:45 a.m. ET
intraday print of **December COMEX futures**, not spot. It was adopted "conservatively"
from a dispersion range — but the dispersion was not noise between competing quotes of the
same thing, it was **three different instruments being compared as if they were one**
(futures open $4,298.30, futures intraday $4,411.70, spot ~$4,356). Corrected marks:

| | Price USD/oz |
|---|---:|
| Spot, Fri 2026-08-07 | **~4,343 – 4,357** |
| Spot, Sat 2026-08-08 | **4,341.78** |
| Week | **+5%** — best weekly close since January |

**(b) The framing has been wrong for longer, and this is the part that matters.** Recent
briefs have described gold in terms of records and rallies. The actual position:

> **Gold peaked at $5,589.38 on 2026-01-28 and is now ~$4,342 — roughly 22% below the
> all-time high.** It fell as far as ~$4,046 in July, a drawdown of ~28% and the steepest
> quarterly correction since 2013.

Last week's +5% is a **bounce inside a deep correction**, not a resumption of the bull
market, and it has not been presented that way. Tho's gold sleeve is his **largest single
position (~35% of the book)** and this is the single most important thing about it.

Drivers of the correction, per source: a hawkish Fed under Chair Warsh; the Iran conflict
acting *bearishly* on gold by lifting oil, inflation expectations and rate projections; and
profit-taking after gold's 60%+ gain in 2025. The recent bounce is attributed to falling
oil, a softer dollar and the weak July payrolls print.

**Forecast dispersion is enormous and should be treated as such** — J.P. Morgan sees
$6,000/oz by year-end and $6,300 in 2027; Morgan Stanley frames a path to $5,200; other
analysts frame the same tape as a broken bull market. Structural support is real (central
banks bought 244t in Q1-2026; the ECB notes gold has passed US Treasuries as the largest
global reserve asset). **No view is adopted here.** The honest summary is that a 22%
drawdown and a $6,000 target are both live, which is itself the risk statement for a 35%
position.

**The NAV/unit problem persists:** the series carries NAV per unit with no unit count, so
the gold sleeve is still **excluded from portfolio totals**. Given the above, this is no
longer a bookkeeping nit — the largest position in the book is the one the system cannot
value.
