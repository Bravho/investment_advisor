## 1. Headline — the baht, not the barrel

The decisive item tonight is **not** Hormuz. It is that the **Thai baht hit a 13–15 month low**,
trading **33.39–33.64** against the dollar versus **33.09** the previous session, and is now down
roughly **5.5% year-to-date**. The stated cause chain is specific and it matters for this portfolio:

```
high global oil price
  -> Thailand's energy import bill rises (structural net energy importer)
  -> current account deteriorates
  -> THB depreciates
```

compounded by tourism entering its **off-peak season**, export momentum to the US and China
**moderating**, and the **BOT policy rate pinned at 1.00%** against a Fed that has not yet cut —
a rate differential that pundits expect to persist for at least a year. Bangkok Post reports
**capital fleeing Thai stocks** alongside the currency move; foreign investors sold a reported
**THB 6.7bn** of Thai stocks and bonds while quietly accumulating oil, retail and tourism names.

Counterweight worth keeping: the **BOT raised its 2026 GDP forecast to 2.3% from 1.5%**, citing
strong exports, government stimulus and easing Middle East tension. The currency and the growth
forecast are pointing in different directions.

**Why this is the headline for Tho specifically:** the project has always treated FX as a *cost*
(`foreign_access_cost.py`) rather than as an *exposure*. Tonight's new module inverts that. See §6.

**The honest reading of that table:** the whole spread is smaller than a single day's equity move.
FX is a *real but second-order* driver of this book ex-gold, and a *first-order* driver only of the
gold sleeve. This is a framing correction, not a trade.

**What it does NOT change:** the **100% TH country flag stands**. A Thai political, regulatory,
SET-liquidity or sovereign event hits all four positions regardless of which currency the revenue
is denominated in. Look-through FX exposure and country concentration are different risks.

**The self-hedge, and where it breaks:** high oil weakens the baht, which lifts the gold sleeve and
PTTEP's translated earnings — so high oil is substantially self-hedging *for this portfolio* even
though it is clearly negative *for Thailand*. The hedge fails on a **demand-led** oil collapse:
PTTEP earnings fall, the baht does not strengthen (risk-off usually means dollar strength), and
gold depends entirely on whether the Fed is cutting. That is the scenario where every leg loses at once.
