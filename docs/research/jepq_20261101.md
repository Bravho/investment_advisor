## 4. Tax for a Thai resident
**US withholding**
- The US–Thailand treaty (Art. 10) sets 15% on dividends; the 10% rate applies only to companies holding at least 10%. A RIC such as JEPQ gets 15%. Without a W-8BEN on file, the rate is 30%.
- Sources: [treaty](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/thailand.pdf), [IRS Table 1](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-lbi/tax-treaty-table-1.pdf).
- JEPQ's payout is mostly ordinary income, so the full distribution is withheld:
  - about 11.6–12% gross becomes **about 9.9–10.2% net** at 15%;
  - it becomes about 8.1–8.4% at 30%.
- That costs roughly **1.7–1.8% a year** at 15%, which is far more than the 0.35% fee.

**Thai tax on remittance**
- Under Por. 161/2566, foreign income earned from 1 Jan 2024 is taxable whenever it is brought into Thailand, for anyone resident 180 days or more. The US tax already paid can be credited under the treaty.
- Sources: [Por.161](https://www.rd.go.th/fileadmin/user%5Fupload/kormor/newlaw/dn161A.pdf), [RD guide](https://www.rd.go.th/fileadmin/user_upload/porphor/GuideTaxFromAbroad_TH.pdf).
- If your marginal rate is above 15%, the gap is added tax when you remit.

**US estate tax**
- A non-US person's US-situs assets above **$60,000** are subject to US estate tax, and US ETF shares count as US-situs. [IRS FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/frequently-asked-questions-on-estate-taxes-for-nonresidents-not-citizens-of-the-united-states)
- An Irish UCITS fund and a Thai feeder fund are generally **not** US-situs.

**UCITS JEPQ LN**
- US withholding applies only to the underlying stock dividends, at 15% inside the Irish fund. The option premium is not subject to US withholding. Ireland generally does not withhold on distributions to non-residents.
- Fund-level leakage is therefore only about 0.1%/yr, an estimate to verify. Thai remittance tax still applies if the income is brought into Thailand.

**Thai feeder funds**
- Thai individuals normally pay no tax on unit-redemption or auto-redemption gains.
- There is no US estate-tax exposure.
- The cost is the Thai fund's fee layer, which comes on top of the master fund's 0.35%.

## 7. Verdict
- **Interesting only as an income product.** It offers a high monthly payout (about 11–12%, variable) with about 0.7× the volatility of the Nasdaq.
- Tho is a total-return investor and does not need the income, so for him it is **less attractive than plain QQQ/SPY or an index fund**.
- If he wants it anyway:
  - **Small amounts in THB:** an **unhedged Thai feeder**, either TLNDQINCOME-UH-X (lowest TER at 0.89%) or KT-JEPQ (0.94%, auto-redemption, minimum 1 THB). Gains are tax-free locally and there is no US estate tax.
  - **Large amounts kept offshore:** **UCITS JEPQ LN via IBKR**, which has the lowest tax leakage and no US estate tax.
  - **Avoid US-listed JEPQ** for a Thai resident. The 15% withholding on an 11% distribution costs about 1.7%/yr, and it carries US estate-tax exposure.
- Hedged feeder classes remove the FX swing but have trailed the unhedged classes recently: 1-year +7.8% against +15.7% for Eastspring.

## 8. Follow-up t42u (1 Nov 2026 ICT): how many layers in each Thai feeder, and what each layer costs
**Short answer:** none of these funds has more than two layers.
- KT-JEPQ, TLNDQINCOME-UH-X (Talis), SCBNDQPINUH, ES-NDQPIN-UH and M-JEPQ are all **two layers**: Thai feeder → the **Irish UCITS JEPQ**. That Irish fund holds the stocks and sells the call options itself.
- None of them is a fund-of-funds, and none buys the US-listed JEPQ or a Luxembourg fund.
- The JPMorgan Global Equity Premium Income fund (JEPG) is a different product, and none of these funds uses it.
- Talis is therefore exactly as "layered" as KT-JEPQ.

### 8.2 Hidden layer: withholding tax at the master fund
- All of these feeders hold the **Irish** UCITS fund, not the US-listed JEPQ.
  - The US withholds 15% only on the underlying stock dividends (Nasdaq-100 dividend yield is roughly 0.6–0.7%), which costs about **0.1%/yr**.
  - Option premium is not subject to US withholding.
  - Ireland does not withhold on payments to non-Irish holders.
  - The Thai fund itself pays no tax.
- If a Thai fund held the US-listed JEPQ instead, 15–30% would be withheld on the full ~11–12% distribution, about **1.7–3.5%/yr**. The feeders avoid this.
- **The 15% leakage applies only when buying US JEPQ directly through Dime!, Webull or IBKR.**

### 8.4 Other effects of the extra layer
- **FX conversion:** the fund converts THB↔USD for every inflow and outflow, at an estimated 0.05–0.2% spread. All holders bear it through the NAV.
- **NAV timing:** the NAV for day T uses the master's London close on T and is published around T+1. Orders are forward-priced, so the buyer does not know the price when placing them. Thai, UK and US holidays mean some days have no trading. Redemption cash arrives T+3 (up to 5–6 business days).
- **Payouts:**
  - None of the unhedged Thai classes passes on the monthly distribution as a dividend. It is reinvested inside the fund, or the fund buys the acc class.
  - Income arrives through **auto-redemption** (KT-JEPQ, ES-UH-A, M-JEPQ), which is a capital gain and normally **tax-free** for Thai individuals.
  - A dividend class (e.g. SCBNDQPIN(D), hedged) pays dividends subject to **10% Thai withholding**.
- **Liquidity and closure:** the Thai AMC may switch to another master fund, change share class or currency, or close the fund. Talis's report states this right explicitly.
  - Sizes: KT 444M, Talis UH 392M, SCB UH 572M, M-JEPQ 2,000M at IPO, Talis hedged only 51M.
- **Plus of the layer:** buying in THB with no US estate tax, no W-8BEN, no remittance tax and no offshore account.

### 8.6 Cheapest-layer option
- **Cheapest overall:** UCITS JEPQ LN (dist) or JEQA LN (acc) bought directly through IBKR, if the money stays offshore. It costs about 0.45%/yr, with no US estate tax.
  - Bringing income or gains back into Thailand can make it more expensive than a feeder.
- **Cheapest Thai route today:**
  - TLNDQINCOME-UH-X: 1.24% all-in, front-end 0.32%, accumulating, minimum 1,000 THB.
  - KT-JEPQ: 1.29%, front-end 0.32%, minimum 1 THB, with tax-free monthly auto-redemption.
  - **M-JEPQ may become the cheapest** (management fee 0.50%) once its first factsheet confirms the TER. Verify before choosing.
- **Avoid for cost:** ES-NDQPIN and SCBNDQPIN (1% front-end fee and higher TER) and the US-listed JEPQ (15% withholding).
