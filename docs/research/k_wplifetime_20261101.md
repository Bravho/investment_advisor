## 4. Pros and cons
**Pros**
- Diversified across income assets (bonds, high-dividend stocks, REITs) in Thailand plus abroad, with a JPMorgan-built model.
- TER of 0.70% is reasonable for a multi-asset fund of funds, and there is no back-end fee.
- Auto-redemption gives a monthly-style cash flow without dividend withholding tax. For a Thai individual, a gain on redeeming mutual-fund units is normally tax-exempt, whereas a fund dividend carries 10% withholding. **Confirm with the Revenue Dept / KAsset.**
- Daily liquidity (T+5).

**Cons**
- **Brand new:** no live performance, volatility or drawdown data; small AUM (59M THB); the actual allocation and hedge ratio are not yet published.
- Payouts can eat principal. In a falling market, auto-redemption sells more units at low prices.
- **No tax deduction** (not RMF or ThaiESG).
- 35% Thai corporate bonds + 13% Thai high-dividend equity + 7.5% Thai REITs = a large Thai/credit tilt. The 7% high-yield and REITs are rate- and credit-sensitive.
- Discretionary FX hedging on about 45% foreign assets, so the currency risk is unknown until factsheets show the hedge %.
- The model's backtest is only 3–5% p.a. before fees, so roughly 2.3–4.3% after the 0.70% TER.
- High 50,000 THB first-purchase minimum at KAsset.

## 5. Who it suits
- **Suits:** a retiree or near-retiree who wants a simple, hands-off cash flow from a moderate-risk mix, accepts FX and market swings, and has a horizon of 5 years or more. These are KAsset's own suitability notes.
- **Less suited:** someone who manages positions actively and doesn't need monthly withdrawals; someone who wants a tax deduction; someone who wants a guaranteed income.
